# ✍️ SECTION 6 — ENGLISH GRAMMAR (Infosys SE OA Level)
### 20 questions • Target time: 20 minutes for the real 5-question section

The English Grammar block is **5 questions in 10 minutes** and tests a narrow set of rules — **subject-verb agreement, tense, prepositions, articles, and error spotting**. Infosys reuses the *same* handful of rules again and again, so this section is the easiest in the whole OA to make 100% correct.

---

### Q1. Choose the correct option: "Neither of the managers ______ present at the meeting yesterday."

A) were  B) was  C) are  D) have been

✅ **Answer: B) was**

**Rule:** **Neither / Either / Each / Every / Everyone / Nobody / One** are **always SINGULAR**, even when followed by "of the + plural noun".

**Step by step:**
1. The subject is **"Neither"**, not "managers" — the phrase *"of the managers"* is only additional information.
2. Singular subject + past time reference ("yesterday") → singular past verb = **was**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The NAEE rule: Neither, Any-of, Either, Each = singular"**
> Think **"NEITHER is ONE"**. Every one of these words means **one single person/thing**:
> - Neither of the boys **is**…
> - Each of the girls **has**…
> - One of my friends **was**…
> **The "of the" phrase is a decoy.** Cover it with your finger, and the verb choice becomes obvious: *"Neither ___ present"* → was/were → **was**.
> **Memory line: "Hide the 'of-phrase' and the answer appears."**

### Q2. "Each of the students ______ submitted the assignment on time."

A) have  B) has  C) are  D) were

✅ **Answer: B) has**

**Step by step:**
1. "Each" is singular (see Q1) → the verb must be singular.
2. Present perfect with a singular subject → **has submitted**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Each = every single one, so the verb stays single"**
> **Each, Every, Either, Neither, One, Many a, Everybody, Nobody** → **singular verb**.
> Note the special one: **"Many a student HAS"** (even though it means many students).
> **Memory line: "Each, Every, Many-a, Neither, Either → verb is a loner."**

### Q3. "The number of applicants ______ increased sharply this year."

A) have  B) has  C) were  D) are

✅ **Answer: B) has**

**Step by step:**
1. **"The number of"** refers to a single **number** → singular.
2. So the verb is **has** (the number has increased).
3. Compare with **"A number of applicants HAVE increased"** — here "a number of" means "many", which is **plural**. Q4 tests exactly this contrast.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "THE number = one; A number = many"**
> | Phrase | Verb |
> |---|---|
> | **The number of** students **is**… | singular (it is one number) |
> | **A number of** students **are**… | plural (it means "many") |
> **Memory line: "THE is one, A is many."** This exact pair appears in Infosys every drive — it is almost a guaranteed mark.

### Q4. "A number of students ______ absent from the class today."

A) was  B) is  C) were  D) has been

✅ **Answer: C) were**

**Step by step:**
1. "A number of students" = **many students** → plural subject.
2. Plural subject with a past time marker ("today" here implies the class that was held) → plural verb = **were**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Quantity words cheat-sheet"**
> | Expression | Number | Verb |
> |---|---|---|
> | A number of | plural | are/were |
> | The number of | singular | is/was |
> | A lot of / Lots of | plural | are |
> | Plenty of | plural | are |
> | Amount of (uncountable) | singular | is |
> **Memory line: "Countable crowds are plural; a countable label is singular."**

---

### Q5. "Ten kilometres ______ a long distance to walk to college."

A) are  B) were  C) is  D) have been

✅ **Answer: C) is**

**Step by step:**
1. "Ten kilometres" looks plural, but it is being treated as **one single measurement** (a distance).
2. **Distances, periods of time, sums of money and weights are treated as SINGULAR** when they are one unit.
3. So → **is**.

**Compare:** *"Ten kilometres **were** covered by the team in two days"* → here they are counted as separate units → plural is correct.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "One amount = one verb (DTMW)"**
> **D**istance, **T**ime, **M**oney, **W**eight → **singular verb**:
> - Ten kilometres **is**…
> - Five years **is** a long time…
> - Fifty rupees **is** not enough…
> - Twenty kilos **is** too heavy…
> **Memory line: "DTMW treats many as one."**

### Q6. "She is good ______ mathematics, but weak in physics."

A) in  B) at  C) with  D) for

✅ **Answer: B) at**

**Step by step:**
1. **"Good at"** is the fixed collocation for a **skill or subject**: good at maths, good at cricket, good at coding.
2. "Weak in" is the standard partner for the second half ("weak in physics") — both are fixed pairs.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Learn preposition PAIRS, not prepositions"**
> **Skill pairs:** good **at**, bad **at**, weak **in**, strong **in**, proficient **in**, expert **in**, excel **in/at**.
> **Feeling pairs:** afraid **of**, angry **with** (a person), angry **at** (a thing), fond **of**, proud **of**, jealous **of**, sorry **for**.
> **Dependence pairs:** depend **on**, rely **on**, insist **on**, consist **of**, comply **with**.
> **Memory line: "Prepositions come in fixed couples — never invent a new partner."** In the OA, the wrong option is usually a *sensible-sounding* preposition like "in" here.

### Q7. "He has been working in this company ______ 2019."

A) for  B) from  C) since  D) by

✅ **Answer: C) since**

**Step by step:**
1. "2019" is a **point of time** (a starting moment) → use **since**.
2. "For" is used with a **duration** ("for five years", "for two months").

**Check:** "He has been working here **for** six years" ✔ (duration) / "**since** 2019" ✔ (point).

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Since = Starting point (S for S); For = Full length (F for F)"**
> - **S**ince → **S**tarting point: since Monday, since 9 a.m., since 2019, since childhood.
> - **F**or → **F**ixed duration: for two hours, for five years, for a long time.
> **Ultra-fast test:** does the phrase mention **a date / clock time / day / month / year**? → **since**. Does it mention **a length of time (a number + hours/days/years)**? → **for**.
> **Memory line: "Since a moment, for a measure."**

---

### Q8. "The train will arrive ______ two hours."

A) after  B) since  C) in  D) within

✅ **Answer: C) in**

**Step by step:**
1. With a **future tense** and a duration, **"in"** means *after that much time from now* → "will arrive in two hours".
2. "After two hours" is also grammatically possible but is used with a **point reference** and is **not** the standard answer when a future action is expressed from the present moment. OA questions use **"in + duration"** for future actions.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "In = from now; After = from then"**
> - **in two hours** → two hours from **now** (present perspective).
> - **after two hours** → two hours from **another event** (past/future reference point).
> - **within two hours** → before two hours have finished (a deadline).
> **Memory line: "Future from NOW = in."**
> Also remember: **in** is used for **months/years/seasons** (in July, in 2026), **on** for **days and dates** (on Monday, on 5 June), **at** for **clock times and night** (at 5 p.m., at night) — **"in-on-at: big to small."**

### Q9. "I am not accustomed ______ such rude behaviour."

A) with  B) for  C) to  D) of

✅ **Answer: C) to**

**Step by step:**
1. **"Accustomed to"** is a fixed collocation.
2. Note the pattern: here **"to" is a preposition**, so a **noun/gerund** follows → "accustomed to **such behaviour**" ✔ / "accustomed to **working** late" ✔

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The 'TO + noun/ing' family"**
> Many words ending in the idea of *habit / relation / comparison* take **to + ing**:
> - accustomed **to**, used **to**, addicted **to**, averse **to**, prone **to**, look forward **to**, object **to**, dedicated **to**
> **Never write "used to working"? — Careful:** *"I am used to **working** late"* ✔ (habit) BUT *"I used to **work** late"* ✔ (past habit, no "ing", no "am").
> **Memory line: "'to' after these words is a bridge to a noun, not an infinitive."**

### Q10. "He is ______ honest man and ______ university student."

A) a, a  B) an, a  C) an, an  D) the, a

✅ **Answer: B) an, a**

**Step by step:**
1. **"honest"** begins with a **vowel SOUND** (the "h" is silent → "onest") → use **an**.
2. **"university"** begins with a **consonant SOUND** ("yoo-niversity") → use **a**.
3. So: an honest man, a university student.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "A/An depends on the SOUND, not the letter"**
> **Take "an" (vowel sound):** an hour, an honest man, an heir, an MBA, an SBI officer, an MLA, an X-ray.
> **Take "a" (consonant sound):** a university, a European, a one-rupee coin, a unique idea, a useful tool.
> **Memory line: "Silent H and vowel letters that sound like 'you' or 'wa' → judge with your EAR, not your eye."**
> Trick for the exam: **say the word aloud in your head** — if you hear a vowel at the start, write "an".

### Q11. "When I reached the station, the train ______ already ______."

A) has, left  B) had, left  C) was, leaving  D) have, left

✅ **Answer: B) had, left**

**Step by step:**
1. Two past actions occur in sequence: first the **train left**, then **I reached** the station.
2. The **earlier** action takes the **past perfect** ("had + past participle"), and the later action takes the simple past.
3. So: When I **reached** (simple past)… the train **had left** (past perfect).

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "First action = HAD; second action = simple past"**
> When two past events are given in one sentence, ask **"which one happened FIRST?"** → that one gets **had + V3**.
> - *The train **had** left **before** I reached.* ✔
> - *I reached **after** the train **had** left.* ✔
> **Memory line: "Earlier past wears HAD."**

### Q12. "She ______ in Chennai since 2015 and knows the city very well."

A) lives  B) lived  C) has been living  D) is living

✅ **Answer: C) has been living**

**Step by step:**
1. The action **started in the past (2015)** and **continues into the present** → present perfect continuous = **has/have been + V-ing**.
2. The marker word **"since"** (with "for") is the strongest signal for the perfect continuous tense.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Since/For + ongoing action = HAS BEEN + ING"**
> Spot the pattern in one glance:
> | Sentence shape | Tense |
> |---|---|
> | `since / for` + still true now | **has/have been + ing** |
> | `already / just / yet` | **has/have + V3** |
> | `yesterday / last year / in 2010` | **simple past** |
> | `while + past continuous` | **was/were + ing** |
> **Memory line: "Since and For → the action is still on the floor."**

### Q13. **Spotting the error:** "One of my friend / is going / to Delhi / tomorrow."

A) One of my friend  B) is going  C) to Delhi  D) tomorrow  E) No error

✅ **Answer: A) "One of my friend"** — it should be **"One of my friends"**.

**Step by step:**
1. The structure **"One of the / my + noun"** is always followed by a **PLURAL noun** (because "one" is chosen *from a group of many*).
2. So → One of my **friends** ✔ (not "friend").
3. Note that the **verb stays singular** because the real subject is "One": *One of my friends **is** going* ✔

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "ONE OF + plural noun, singular verb"**
> You cannot be "one of" a single thing — you must be one **of many**. Hence:
> - One of my **friends** **is**…
> - One of the **boys** **was**…
> - One of the **reasons** **is**…
> **Memory line: "'One of' needs a crowd, but takes a single verb."**
> In error-spotting questions, **"one of + singular noun" is the single most-planted error** in Infosys papers.

### Q14. **Spotting the error:** "He is senior / than me / in the / office."

A) He is senior  B) than me  C) in the  D) office  E) No error

✅ **Answer: B) "than me"** — it should be **"to me"** (senior **to** me).

**Step by step:**
1. **Latin comparatives** (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, preferable, elder) do **not** use **than**; they take **to**.
2. So → *He is senior **to** me in the office* ✔

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "SJSPPE takes TO, not THAN"**
> **S**enior, **J**unior, **S**uperior, **P**rior, **P**referable, **E**lder/Exterior/Interior → **to**
> - senior **to**, junior **to**, superior **to**, inferior **to**, prior **to**, preferable **to**, elder **to**
> **Compare with normal comparatives**, which DO take "than": taller **than**, better **than**, more intelligent **than**.
> **Memory line: "Latin words are snobs — they only accept TO."**
> Second trap in the same question: after "than", if you use a pronoun, formal English prefers **"than I"** (than I am) in writing — but the OA's tested error here is **than → to**.

### Q15. **Sentence correction:** "The teacher along with the students were present in the hall."

A) were present in the hall
B) was present in the hall
C) have been present in the hall
D) were being present in the hall

✅ **Answer: B) was present in the hall**

**Step by step:**
1. Phrases such as **along with, as well as, together with, accompanied by, in addition to, besides** **do not change** the number of the subject.
2. The real subject here is **"The teacher"** (singular) → the verb must be **singular** → **was**.
3. So: *The teacher, along with the students, **was** present in the hall* ✔

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The parenthetical phrase is invisible"**
> Words that make the verb agree with the **FIRST** subject only:
> **along with, as well as, together with, accompanied by, in addition to, including, besides.**
> **How to apply it in 3 seconds:** mentally put the phrase in commas (or delete it) → "The teacher, ~~along with the students~~, **was** present."
> **Compare with "AND"**, which makes the subject plural: *The teacher **and** the students **were** present* ✔
> **Memory line: "With-phrases don't add; AND adds."**

### Q16. "Hardly had I reached the station ______ the train left."

A) than  B) when  C) then  D) that

✅ **Answer: B) when**

**Step by step:**
1. The fixed pairs of these "inversion" structures are:
   - **Hardly had … WHEN**
   - **Scarcely had … WHEN / BEFORE**
   - **No sooner had … THAN**
2. So: "Hardly had I reached the station **when** the train left" ✔

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "No sooner → THAN; everything else → WHEN"**
> - **No sooner … THAN** (the two **N**'s and **T** travel together)
> - **Hardly / Scarcely … WHEN (or BEFORE)**
> Also remember the **inversion** (verb before subject) that comes with these words: *"Hardly **had I** reached…"*, *"No sooner **had he** left…"*. If you see "Hardly I had", that is the error.
> **Memory line: "No-sooner-THAN, hardly-WHEN."**

### Q17. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word: "The manager's decision was ______; nobody in the team could argue against it."

A) impeccable  B) irrefutable  C) illogical  D) inevitable

✅ **Answer: B) irrefutable**

**Step by step:**
1. The clue phrase is **"nobody could argue against it"** → the decision cannot be **refuted** (proved wrong) → **irrefutable**.
2. Word breakdown: **ir-** (not) + **refute** (prove wrong) + **-able** → "cannot be disproved".

**Why the others fail:**
- **impeccable** = flawless (used for behaviour/quality, not for an argument)
- **illogical** = against logic (the opposite of what is needed here)
- **inevitable** = unavoidable (about certainty of happening, not about argument)

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Read the CLUE, then match the prefix"**
> Vocabulary fill-in-the-blanks always contain a **signal phrase** ("nobody could argue against it"). Convert the signal into plain words, then pick the option with that meaning.
> **Prefix decoding:**
> - **ir- / in- / im- / un- / dis-** = **not**
> → irrefutable (cannot be refuted), impeccable (without flaw), inevitable (cannot be avoided), illogical (not logical)
> **Memory line: "Clue first, prefix second."**

### Q18. "If I ______ a bird, I would fly across the ocean."

A) am  B) was  C) were  D) will be

✅ **Answer: C) were**

**Step by step:**
1. This is an **imaginary / unreal** condition — it is not true and cannot become true.
2. English uses the **subjunctive mood** for unreal conditions: for **all subjects** (I, he, she, it, they) use **WERE**.
3. Structure: **If + were …, would + V1** → "If I **were** a bird, I **would** fly."

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Unreal = WERE, for everybody"**
> | Condition type | Structure |
> |---|---|
> | Possible / real | If it **rains**, I **will** stay |
> | Imaginary (present) | If I **were** rich, I **would** travel |
> | Imaginary (past) | If I **had studied**, I **would have passed** |
> **Memory line: "WERE means wishful; WAS means it was real."**
> Classic exam sentence: *"If I **were** you, I would…"* — never "If I was you" in formal writing.

### Q19. **Sentence correction:** "She likes dancing, singing and to paint in her free time."

A) and to paint in her free time
B) and painting in her free time
C) and paints in her free time
D) and paint in her free time

✅ **Answer: B) and painting in her free time**

**Step by step:**
1. This is a **list**, so all items must be in the **same grammatical form** — the rule of **parallelism**.
2. The first two items are gerunds: **dancing, singing** → the third must also be a gerund → **painting**.
3. Correct sentence: *She likes **dancing**, **singing** and **painting** in her free time.*

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "In a list, keep the same SHAPE"**
> Parallelism checklist — the items must match in form:
> - gerund with gerund: dancing, singing, **painting**
> - infinitive with infinitive: to dance, to sing, **to paint**
> - noun with noun: music, dance, **painting**
> **Pairs Infosys loves to make non-parallel:** *not only … **but also***, *either … **or***, *neither … **nor***, *both … **and***.
> **Memory line: "One list, one uniform."**

### Q20. Choose the correct indirect form: He said, "I am busy now."

A) He said that he is busy now
B) He said that he was busy then
C) He said that I was busy then
D) He says that he was busy now

✅ **Answer: B) He said that he was busy then**

**Step by step (the changes in reported speech):**
1. The reporting verb **"said"** is in the past → the reported verb moves **one step back**: "am" → **was**.
2. Pronoun changes to suit the reporter: **"I"** (the speaker) → **"he"**.
3. Time word changes: **"now" → "then"**.
4. "that" is added before the reported clause → **He said that he was busy then** ✔

**Standard conversion table (memorise):**
| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| am / is | was |
| are | were |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tomorrow | the next day |
| yesterday | the previous day |
| this | that |
| here | there |

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Tense back, pronoun flip, clock change — in that order"**
> Three moves, always in the same sequence:
> 1. **Tense steps back** one place (present → past → past perfect).
> 2. **Pronoun flips** to match the new speaker.
> 3. **Time and place words change** (now → then, today → that day, here → there).
> **Memory line: "Back one step, flip the pronoun, reset the clock."**

## 📌 Section 6 — The 10 Rules That Cover Almost Every Infosys Grammar Question

| # | Rule | Instant signal in the question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Neither / Either / Each / Every / One → **singular verb** | "of the + plural noun" is the decoy |
| 2 | **THE number → has**, **A number → have** | "the number of" vs "a number of" |
| 3 | **One of + plural noun**, singular verb | "one of" |
| 4 | along with / as well as / together with → verb follows the **first** subject | comma phrases |
| 5 | Distance / Time / Money / Weight → **singular verb** | ten kilometres, five years |
| 6 | **since** (point of time) vs **for** (duration) | a year or date → since |
| 7 | senior / junior / superior / inferior / prior / preferable → **to**, not than | Latin comparatives |
| 8 | Hardly → **when**; No sooner → **than**; Scarcely → when/before | inversion with "had" |
| 9 | Unreal condition → **were** for all subjects | "If I ___ you" |
| 10 | Lists must be **parallel** | X, Y and Z |
