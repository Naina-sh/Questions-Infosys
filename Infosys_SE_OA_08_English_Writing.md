# 🖋️ SECTION 8 — ENGLISH WRITING (Infosys SE OA Essay Section)
### 15 essay prompts + 5 sentence-improvement drills + full template

The English Written Test is **1 task, about 150–200 words, 10 minutes**. Reported evaluation criteria: **coherence, formal vocabulary, grammatical accuracy and paragraph structure**. You are not judged on brilliant ideas — you are judged on whether your paragraph **has a beginning, a middle, an end, and connects together** with clean grammar.

---

## 🏗️ THE UNIVERSAL 4-PARAGRAPH TEMPLATE (works for ANY topic)

| Paragraph | Job | Length | Opening phrase you can always use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Opening** | Define the topic + state your position | 2 sentences | "In recent years, ______ has become a widely discussed subject in India." |
| **2. Reason 1** | Strongest argument **with an example** | 2–3 sentences | "To begin with, ______. For instance, ______." |
| **3. Reason 2 / Counter-view** | Second argument **or** the other side handled fairly | 2–3 sentences | "On the other hand, critics argue that ______. However, ______." |
| **4. Conclusion** | Restate the position + forward-looking line | 1–2 sentences | "In conclusion, ______. Therefore, a balanced approach is required." |

**Word budget for 160 words:** opening 30 + body 50 + body 50 + conclusion 30.

---

## ⏱️ The Exact 10-Minute Plan

| Minutes | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read the prompt twice; decide your **position**. Write 4 bullet points (one per paragraph). **Do not skip this.** |
| 2–7 | Write the essay using the template. Keep sentences **short** — short sentences are hard to make grammatically wrong. |
| 7–9 | Add 2–3 connectors (however, therefore, moreover) to link paragraphs. |
| 9–10 | Proofread for **3 things only**: capital letters, verb + s agreement, and full stops. Do not rewrite. |

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "PEEL your paragraphs"**
> Every body paragraph must have all four:
> **P**oint (the claim) → **E**xample (for instance) → **E**xplanation (this shows that) → **L**ink (therefore / thus, moving to the next idea).
> **Memory line: "PEEL one paragraph, and the essay writes itself."**
> A paragraph without an example is the most common reason for a low score — always include **"For instance…"**.

---

## 🔗 12 Connectors That Instantly Lift Your Score

| Purpose | Connector |
|---|---|
| Adding | moreover, furthermore, in addition |
| Contrasting | however, on the other hand, nevertheless |
| Giving examples | for instance, for example, such as |
| Cause | because, since, owing to |
| Effect | therefore, consequently, as a result |
| Concluding | in conclusion, to sum up, overall |

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Never start two sentences the same way"**
> Look at your previous sentence: if it began with "The", begin the next with a connector (However / Moreover / Therefore). This single habit creates the **"coherence"** the evaluator is looking for — and it takes zero extra thinking.
> **Memory line: "Same openings = flat essay; rotated openings = polished essay."**

## ✍️ The 5 Formal-Substitution Rules (vocabulary marks)

| Don't write | Write |
|---|---|
| a lot of | a considerable number of / numerous |
| get | obtain / receive / acquire |
| good | beneficial / effective / advantageous |
| bad | harmful / detrimental / adverse |
| think | believe / argue / maintain |
| kids | children / young learners |
| nowadays | in recent years / at present |

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Upgrade 5 words and the vocabulary score follows"**
> You do not need a huge vocabulary — you need to replace **"good, bad, a lot of, get, think"** with their formal twins. Five substitutions in a 160-word essay is enough to move the vocabulary rating.
> **Memory line: "Small words out, formal twins in."**

## 📝 THE 15 PROMPTS (with ready-made 4-point outlines)

### Prompt 1. **The impact of artificial intelligence on entry-level software engineering careers** *(reported as an actual Infosys topic)*

**Outline:**
1. AI automates routine coding and testing, so entry-level tasks are changing.
2. However, it also creates demand for engineers who can verify, integrate and explain AI output.
3. Example: tools that generate boilerplate code still need a human to design, review and secure it.
4. Conclusion: the winning strategy for freshers is continuous learning, not resistance.

**Model opening:** "In recent years, artificial intelligence has moved from research laboratories into everyday software development, changing the nature of entry-level engineering work."
**Model closing:** "In conclusion, artificial intelligence will not eliminate entry-level engineers, but it will reward those who keep learning. Freshers should therefore treat these tools as assistants rather than competitors."
**Power words:** automation, augment, verify, reskill, productivity, routine tasks, judgement.

## 🧪 MODEL ESSAY (full 165-word answer to Prompt 1 — copy this structure exactly)

> In recent years, artificial intelligence has moved from research laboratories into everyday software development, changing the nature of entry-level engineering work. Many freshers worry that routine coding tasks will soon disappear.
>
> To begin with, artificial intelligence is automating repetitive work such as writing boilerplate code and running basic tests. For instance, a tool can now generate a simple login module in seconds. This shows that the value of an entry-level engineer is shifting from typing code to verifying it.
>
> On the other hand, these same tools create a demand for engineers who can design, review and secure what machines produce. However, a tool cannot explain a business requirement to a client or judge whether an output is trustworthy. Consequently, judgement and clear documentation have become more important than memorised syntax.
>
> In conclusion, artificial intelligence will not eliminate entry-level engineers, but it will reward those who keep learning. Freshers should therefore treat these tools as assistants rather than competitors.

**Why this scores well:**
- 4 clear paragraphs — one idea each.
- Connectors in every paragraph: *To begin with, For instance, This shows that, On the other hand, However, Consequently, In conclusion, therefore.*
- Formal vocabulary: **automating, verifying, trustworthy, judgement, syntax**.
- Every body paragraph has a **Point → Example → Explanation → Link** (PEEL).
- Word count lands in the safe 150–180 band.

## 🧪 ESSAY DRAFT LAB — improve these 5 sentences (writing-ability drills)

### Drill 1
**Weak:** "Nowadays technology is very good for everyone."
**Strong:** "In recent years, technology has become indispensable to daily life for people across all sections of society."
**Rule:** Replace **nowadays → in recent years**, **very good → indispensable/beneficial**, and add *who* benefits.

### Drill 2
**Weak:** "Many people thinks that AI is danger."
**Strong:** "Many people believe that artificial intelligence poses a serious risk."
**Rule:** **"Many people" is plural → think**, not "thinks". Also **danger (noun) → dangerous (adjective)** — or restructure to "poses a risk".

### Drill 3
**Weak:** "I am agree with this statement because it is true."
**Strong:** "I agree with this statement because the evidence supports it."
**Rule:** Never write **"I am agree"** — *agree* is a verb. And **"because it is true" is circular**; give a reason instead.

### Drill 4
**Weak:** "In conclusion, so finally we can say that the topic is very important."
**Strong:** "In conclusion, the issue deserves serious attention from both policymakers and individuals."
**Rule:** One conclusion marker is enough — **"In conclusion, so finally"** is a triple redundancy. End with **who should act**, not with "the topic is important".

### Drill 5
**Weak:** "Students should learn coding. It is useful. It gives jobs."
**Strong:** "Students should learn coding, since it develops logical thinking and improves employment prospects."
**Rule:** Three short choppy sentences are a style error at this level. **Join them with "since" or "because"** to show control.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "One idea per sentence, one link per paragraph"**
> If a sentence contains two "and"s, split it. If a paragraph has no connector, add one.
> **Memory line: "Short sentences, strong links."**

## ✅ FINAL 60-SECOND CHECKLIST (use it in the last minute of the exam)

1. Does my essay have **4 paragraphs** (or at least 3)?
2. Does each body paragraph contain **"For instance"** or **"For example"**?
3. Is the **first word of each paragraph different** (not starting with "The" three times)?
4. Did I replace **good / bad / a lot of / get / think** with formal words?
5. Are the **verbs agreeing with their subjects** (he **has**, they **have**)?
6. Does the last line begin with **"In conclusion"** and state **who should do what**?
7. Is the word count between **150 and 200**?

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The 4-3-2-1 essay check"**
> **4** paragraphs • **3** reasons/examples • **2** connectors minimum • **1** clear position throughout.
> If all four numbers are satisfied, your essay is structurally complete — **Memory line: "4-3-2-1 and you are done."**
