# 📖 SECTION 7 — VERBAL ABILITY: RC, PARA JUMBLES & VOCABULARY (Infosys SE OA Level)
### 18 questions • Target time: 18 minutes for the real 20-question section

The reported Verbal Ability block gives **20 questions in 20 minutes** — that is **one minute per question**. So RC questions here are **short and direct** (main idea, direct detail, one inference), followed by para jumbles, synonyms, sentence selection and fill in the blanks. **Never read the passage first; read the questions first, then hunt.**

---

## 📄 PASSAGE A (for Q1–Q3)

> The pace at which artificial intelligence is reshaping the workplace has surprised even its most optimistic advocates. Tasks that once required a team of specialists can now be completed in minutes by a single engineer using the right tools. Yet the same tools that threaten routine employment also create demand for people who can judge, verify and explain machine output. Industry surveys suggest that employers are not hiring fewer engineers; they are hiring engineers with different habits — those who question results, document their reasoning, and accept that half of what they learn in their first year will be obsolete within five years. The real risk, then, is not that machines take over, but that professionals stop learning.

### Q1. Which of the following best expresses the main idea of the passage?

A) Artificial intelligence will eliminate most engineering jobs within five years
B) The main danger of AI is not job loss but the unwillingness of professionals to keep learning
C) Employers now prefer engineers who avoid using AI tools
D) Universities must redesign their syllabi every five years

✅ **Answer: B) The main danger of AI is not job loss but the unwillingness of professionals to keep learning**

**Step by step:**
1. The **last sentence** states the author's conclusion explicitly: *"The real risk, then, is not that machines take over, but that professionals stop learning."*
2. In a single-paragraph passage, the **final line (after "then") usually carries the main idea**.
3. Option A contradicts the passage (the passage says employers are *not* hiring fewer engineers).
4. Option C is the reverse of the passage (the tools create demand for people who *judge* machine output).
5. Option D is not mentioned at all.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Main idea lives in the first and last lines"**
> For a one-paragraph RC:
> 1. Read the **first sentence** (topic) and the **last sentence** (conclusion). 70% of main-idea answers come from those two lines.
> 2. **Eliminate any option containing an extreme word** — "always", "never", "eliminate", "all", "must". Options A and D die instantly here.
> 3. The correct answer must be **neither too broad nor too narrow** — it should cover the whole paragraph in one sentence.
> **Memory line: "First line = topic, last line = verdict, extreme words = trap."**

### Q2. According to the passage, the engineers most in demand are those who

A) can work without any tools
B) question results and document their reasoning
C) avoid routine tasks
D) have more than five years of experience

✅ **Answer: B) question results and document their reasoning**

**Step by step:**
1. The passage says employers are hiring engineers "with different habits — those who **question results, document their reasoning**…".
2. This is a **direct-detail** question: the answer is stated word-for-word in the passage.
3. Options A, C and D are not supported anywhere in the text.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Direct-detail = scan for the KEYWORDS, do not re-read"**
> For any "according to the passage" question:
> 1. Pick the **keyword from the question** ("most in demand" → look for "hiring").
> 2. Scan **only the sentence containing that keyword** (usually the 4th sentence, the one with the examples).
> 3. Choose the option that **matches the passage's wording**, not the option that sounds most logical in real life.
> **Memory line: "Keyword → sentence → exact wording."** Answers that "sound sensible" but are not in the text are always wrong.

### Q3. The expression "surprised even its most optimistic advocates" suggests that

A) the advocates were wrong about AI
B) even those most hopeful about AI underestimated how fast the change would come
C) no one supports AI any longer
D) the changes have been slower than expected

✅ **Answer: B) even those most hopeful about AI underestimated how fast the change would come**

**Step by step:**
1. An "optimistic advocate" is a **supporter** of AI — and if even *they* were **surprised**, the change was **faster or bigger than they expected**.
2. The words "the pace at which…" before the phrase confirm that the surprise is about **speed**.
3. Option D says the opposite (slower). Option C overstates ("no one supports"). Option A is not implied.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Phrase-meaning questions: unpack every word"**
> Split the expression into its parts and translate literally:
> - "advocates" = supporters → the passage is talking about **the pro-AI side**
> - "even" = shows it went **beyond the extreme point** → the change outran the most hopeful expectations
> - "surprised" = unexpected → **faster/bigger** than expected
> **Memory line: "Who is the person? What did they expect? What actually happened?"**
> Also eliminate anything with **absolute words** ("no one", "never") — they are almost always trap options in RC.

## 📄 PASSAGE B (for Q4–Q5)

> Remote work was once promoted as a way to give employees freedom. In practice, many teams discovered that freedom without structure produces isolation. Meetings multiplied, messages accumulated, and the boundary between office hours and personal time dissolved. Companies that handled the shift well did something counter-intuitive: they increased the number of scheduled conversations and made each one shorter. Written updates replaced the casual chat by the coffee machine, forcing people to state their problems precisely. The result was not more control but more clarity — and clarity, it turned out, was the freedom employees had actually wanted.

### Q4. According to the passage, what did the companies that handled remote work well actually do?

A) They reduced the number of meetings
B) They allowed employees to set their own hours
C) They arranged more scheduled conversations of shorter duration
D) They replaced written updates with phone calls

✅ **Answer: C) They arranged more scheduled conversations of shorter duration**

**Step by step:**
1. The passage states: "they **increased the number** of scheduled conversations and **made each one shorter**".
2. Option A is the opposite (meetings increased, not decreased).
3. Option B is a general idea about remote work, not what these companies did.
4. Option D reverses the passage — written updates **replaced** casual chats, not the other way round.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Watch for REVERSED options"**
> RC questions almost always include one option stating the **exact opposite** of the passage (here "reduced the number of meetings" and "replaced written updates with phone calls"). Catch them by checking the **direction of the verb**: *increased vs reduced*, *replaced vs was replaced by*.
> **Memory line: "Check whether the option says the same thing or the opposite thing."**
> **Also use the 'counter-intuitive' flag:** when a passage calls something counter-intuitive, the clause right after it is almost always the answer.

### Q5. The passage implies that the kind of freedom employees truly wanted was

A) the freedom to work from home
B) the freedom from all meetings
C) clarity about what they were expected to do
D) the freedom to choose their own projects

✅ **Answer: C) clarity about what they were expected to do**

**Step by step:**
1. The last sentence says: "**clarity**, it turned out, was the freedom employees had actually wanted."
2. So the "freedom" implied here is **clarity**, not physical location or project choice.
3. Options A and D are plausible in real life but are **not stated or implied** by the passage.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The last line answers the 'implies' question 80% of the time"**
> In an inference question the answer is **never** a fact from outside the passage (how offices normally work, what employees generally like). It must be **directly derivable from the text**.
> **Test every option with: "Can I point to a sentence that says this?"** If you cannot put your finger on the line, it is not the answer.
> **Memory line: "Inference = inside the passage, not outside knowledge."**

### Q6. Arrange the following sentences in the correct order to form a meaningful paragraph.

- **P.** The first is that it demands accuracy: a single wrong digit can change the result entirely.
- **Q.** Learning mathematics is different from learning history.
- **R.** This is why regular practice, rather than last-minute reading, decides the outcome.
- **S.** The second is that it demands patience, because understanding a concept often takes longer than memorising it.

A) QPSR  B) PSRQ  C) PQSR  D) QSPR

✅ **Answer: A) QPSR**

**Step by step:**
1. **Q introduces the topic** — a comparison between mathematics and history. An opening sentence introduces the subject → **Q is first**.
2. **P** says "**The first** is that…" → it must follow the sentence that sets up a list → **P follows Q**.
3. **S** says "**The second** is that…" → it must follow the "first" → **S follows P**.
4. **R** says "**This is why** …" → a conclusion that summarises both reasons → **R is last**.
5. Final order: **Q → P → S → R**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Hunt for the SIGNPOSTS, not the meaning"**
> You do not need to understand the paragraph — you need to spot the signal words:
> | Signal word | Where it goes |
> |---|---|
> | "The first…", "Firstly" | early |
> | "The second…", "Moreover", "Also" | after the first |
> | "This is why", "Therefore", "Thus", "Hence" | **last** |
> | "However", "But", "Yet" | after a contrasting idea |
> | A pronoun ("this", "these", "it", "they") | **after** the noun it refers to |
> **Memory line: "Opening introduces, first/second in order, 'therefore' concludes."** In most para jumbles the **"therefore / this is why" sentence is the last one** and the **general statement is the first**.

### Q7. Arrange the sentences to form a coherent paragraph.

- **P.** However, the ability to say no politely is a skill that can be learned.
- **Q.** Most engineers begin their careers wanting to please everyone around them.
- **R.** Without it, they end up overworked and unable to deliver their best work.
- **S.** It begins with a clear understanding of what one is genuinely responsible for.

A) QRPS  B) QPRS  C) PQRS  D) QSPR

✅ **Answer: A) QRPS**

**Step by step:**
1. **Q** is the general opening statement (engineers starting their careers) → **first**.
2. **R** describes the **problem** that follows ("without it… end up overworked") → **second**.
3. **P** begins with "**However**", signalling a turn from the problem to the solution ("…is a skill that can be learned") → **third**.
4. **S** explains the solution using the pronoun "**It**" (= the ability to say no) → it must come **immediately after P** → **fourth**.
5. Final order: **Q → R → P → S**.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Pronoun chain + contrast pivot"**
> Two rules decide most jumbles:
> 1. **A pronoun cannot appear before its noun.** "It begins with…" must follow the sentence that introduced "the ability to say no".
> 2. **"However / But" marks the middle** — problem first, contrast (solution) second.
> **Memory line: "Situation → problem → 'However' solution → explanation."**

### Q8. Choose the word that is **most similar in meaning** to **MITIGATE**.

A) intensify  B) alleviate  C) estimate  D) justify

✅ **Answer: B) alleviate**

**Step by step:**
1. **Mitigate** = to make something less severe or painful (mitigate the risk, mitigate the damage).
2. **Alleviate** = to make pain or a problem less severe → same meaning ✔
3. **Intensify** = the exact opposite. **Estimate** and **justify** are unrelated.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Group the vocabulary by theme, not alphabet"**
> **"Make it LESS" family:** mitigate, alleviate, assuage, ease, relieve, lessen, allay.
> **"Make it MORE" family:** intensify, aggravate, exacerbate, heighten.
> **Memory line: "MITI-gate and ALLEVI-ate both push the problem DOWN."** When you see one word of a family, immediately think of its opposite family — the antonym is usually an option.
> Also watch the sound link: **"alleviate pain"** is a medical phrase you already know — connect the new word to a phrase you have heard.

### Q9. Choose the word that is **most opposite in meaning** to **CANDID**.

A) frank  B) evasive  C) honest  D) blunt

✅ **Answer: B) evasive**

**Step by step:**
1. **Candid** = open, straightforward, speaking honestly.
2. **Evasive** = avoiding a direct answer, not straightforward → the opposite ✔
3. **Frank**, **honest** and **blunt** are all **synonyms** of candid (they say the same thing) — they cannot be the answer to an *opposite*-meaning question.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "In antonym questions, the synonyms are the decoys"**
> When asked for the **OPPOSITE**, the options usually contain **three synonyms and one antonym**. So:
> 1. First identify which options mean **the same** as the given word (here frank, honest, blunt).
> 2. The remaining option is your answer, even if it looks unfamiliar.
> **Memory line: "Opposite question → delete the look-alikes."**
> **Related family:** candid / frank / blunt / forthright ↔ evasive / guarded / secretive / diplomatic.

### Q10. Five sentences are given below. Four of them form a coherent paragraph; one does **not** fit. Identify the odd sentence.

1. A well-designed interview tests problem solving rather than memory.
2. Candidates who think aloud give the interviewer something to evaluate.
3. Companies that hire on the basis of trivia often lose good engineers in the first year.
4. Most interviewers in the software industry hold a degree in engineering.
5. A short coding exercise reveals far more than an hour of recited definitions.

A) Sentence 2  B) Sentence 3  C) Sentence 4  D) Sentence 5

✅ **Answer: C) Sentence 4**

**Step by step:**
1. Sentences 1, 2, 3 and 5 all discuss the **same argument**: what a good interview should test and the cost of testing the wrong things.
2. Sentence 4 is a **fact about the qualifications of interviewers** — it does not contribute to the argument about *what should be tested*.
3. Therefore sentence 4 is the odd one out.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The odd sentence changes the SUBJECT, not the opinion"**
> In "odd sentence out" questions, the four connected sentences always share a **common subject**, while the odd one introduces a different subject (even if it uses similar words like "interviewer").
> **Test:** cover each sentence in turn and ask, *"Does the paragraph still flow without it?"* If yes, that sentence can be removed — but in these questions, the odd one is the one whose **removal keeps the rest coherent**.
> **Memory line: "Same topic = family; new topic = intruder."**

### Q11. Choose the option that best completes the sentence: "Regular feedback helps employees correct their course early; an annual review, by contrast, often arrives ______."

A) after the damage has already been done
B) at a very convenient time
C) with a great deal of documentation
D) before any problem has arisen

✅ **Answer: A) after the damage has already been done**

**Step by step:**
1. The phrase **"by contrast"** signals that the second half must show the **opposite** of "correct their course early".
2. The opposite of *early correction* is *late information* → "arrives **after the damage has already been done**" ✔
3. Option D states the same as the first half (early), which contradicts "by contrast". Options B and C do not create a contrast at all.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Contrast words decide the blank"**
> Scan the sentence for a contrast marker first: **by contrast, however, but, whereas, on the other hand, unlike, although**.
> Then fill the blank with the **opposite idea** of the first half:
> - first half says *early* → blank must be *late*
> - first half says *cheap* → blank must be *expensive*
> **Memory line: "See 'but', choose the opposite."** This single habit solves sentence-completion questions in under 15 seconds.

### Q12. What does the idiom **"to bite the bullet"** mean?

A) To speak without thinking
B) To endure a painful or difficult situation with courage
C) To take credit for someone else's work
D) To waste an opportunity

✅ **Answer: B) To endure a painful or difficult situation with courage**

**Step by step:**
1. The idiom means to **accept something unpleasant that cannot be avoided** and face it bravely.
2. Example: *"With the deadline approaching, the team had to bite the bullet and work through the weekend."*

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Picture the idiom literally, then move it to the workplace"**
> - **bite the bullet** → imagine a soldier biting a bullet during surgery → **enduring pain**.
> - **burn the midnight oil** → a lamp burning late at night → **working till late**.
> - **hit the nail on the head** → a hammer striking exactly right → **be exactly correct**.
> **Memory line: "If you can draw it, you can recall it."** Create one mental picture per idiom and the meaning sticks far better than a memorised definition.
> **Common OA idioms to know:** *let the cat out of the bag* (reveal a secret), *once in a blue moon* (very rarely), *a blessing in disguise* (hidden benefit), *to pull someone's leg* (to joke with someone), *in hot water* (in trouble).

### Q13. What does **"to burn the midnight oil"** mean?

A) To waste money on unnecessary things
B) To study or work late into the night
C) To become angry very quickly
D) To finish work before the deadline

✅ **Answer: B) To study or work late into the night**

**Step by step:**
1. Before electricity, people burned oil lamps to continue working after dark.
2. So the idiom means **to work or study deep into the night**.
3. Example: *"She burned the midnight oil to complete her project report."*

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Deadline idioms cheat-sheet"**
> | Idiom | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | burn the midnight oil | work late into the night |
> | race against time | hurry to meet a deadline |
> | beat the clock | finish just in time |
> | a race against the clock | same as above |
> | cut corners | do something badly to save time/money |
> | in the nick of time | at the very last moment |
> **Memory line: "Night oil = late work; beat the clock = just in time."**

### Q14. Doctor : Patient :: Teacher : ?

A) School  B) Student  C) Book  D) Principal

✅ **Answer: B) Student**

**Step by step:**
1. A doctor **serves / treats** a patient → the relation is "professional ↔ the person served".
2. A teacher **teaches** a student → the same relation ✔
3. "School" is a place and "book" is a tool — both describe the *setting*, not the *person served*.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Say the relation as a full sentence first"**
> Build a sentence: *"A doctor treats a patient."* Then test each option inside it: *"A teacher teaches a **student**"* ✔ — the other options break the sentence.
> **Common relation types in the OA:**
> - **Worker → workplace** (teacher : school, chef : kitchen)
> - **Worker → tool** (carpenter : saw)
> - **Cause → effect** (rain : flood)
> - **Part → whole** (wheel : car)
> - **Worker → the person served** (doctor : patient) ← this question
> **Memory line: "Sentence first, option second."**

### Q15. Sculptor : Statue :: Architect : ?

A) Building  B) Blueprint  C) Cement  D) Engineer

✅ **Answer: A) Building**

**Step by step:**
1. A sculptor **creates a finished statue** → the relation is "creator → the thing created".
2. An architect **designs a building** → the finished thing created ✔
3. **Blueprint** looks tempting, but a blueprint is the **plan**, not the created product. The sculptor too works from a sketch — yet we chose the *statue*, so consistency demands the *building*, not the plan.
4. **Cement** is a raw material and **engineer** is another person.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "Match the KIND of object, not just the topic"**
> Many analogy options are **related but of the wrong kind**. Before finalising, ask: *"Is my chosen word the same KIND of thing as the second word of the first pair?"*
> - Statue = **finished product** → so choose a **finished product** (building), not a **plan** (blueprint) or a **material** (cement).
> **Memory line: "Same relation AND same kind."**

### Q16. One word for: "A speech delivered without any previous preparation."

A) Extempore  B) Verbose  C) Soliloquy  D) Euphemism

✅ **Answer: A) Extempore**

**Step by step:**
1. A speech given **on the spot, without preparation** = **extempore** (also called *impromptu*).
2. Check the others:
   - **Verbose** = using too many words (a style, not a speech type)
   - **Soliloquy** = a speech made to oneself, especially in a play
   - **Euphemism** = a mild word used instead of a harsh one

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "One-word substitution: learn the 10 that repeat"**
> | Definition | Word |
> |---|---|
> | Speech without preparation | **extempore / impromptu** |
> | Speech made to oneself | **soliloquy** |
> | Speech praising someone who has died | **eulogy** |
> | One who hates mankind | **misanthrope** |
> | One who loves mankind | **philanthropist** |
> | One who studies the earth's structure | **geologist** |
> | One who uses both hands equally well | **ambidextrous** |
> | Something that cannot be avoided | **inevitable** |
> | One who talks too much | **loquacious / garrulous** |
> | A government by the people | **democracy** |
> **Memory line: "Learn definition → word, never word → definition."** OA questions always give the definition and ask for the word.

### Q17. Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word: "The committee decided to ______ the proposal until the next budget was approved."

A) defer  B) deter  C) differ  D) detest

✅ **Answer: A) defer**

**Step by step:**
1. The sentence needs the meaning "to **postpone**" → **defer** = to put off to a later time ✔
2. Check the confusables:
   - **deter** = to discourage someone from acting
   - **differ** = to be different / to disagree
   - **detest** = to hate strongly

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The DE- confusables"**
> | Word | Meaning | Hook |
> |---|---|---|
> | **defer** | postpone | **deF-Er = FuturE** (later) |
> | **deter** | discourage | **deTER = TERror** (scare away) |
> | **differ** | be different / disagree | **diFFer = diFFerent** |
> | **detest** | hate | **deTEST = TEST your hate** |
> **Memory line: "DeFER = Later; deTER = Stop someone; difFER = Not the same; deTEST = Hate."**
> Same family worth knowing: **proceed, succeed, exceed, precede** all take "-ceed"; only **supersede** takes "-sede".

### Q18. Fill in the blank: "Despite the ______ progress, the team remained optimistic about meeting the deadline."

A) painstaking  B) sluggish  C) rigorous  D) remarkable

✅ **Answer: B) sluggish**

**Step by step:**
1. The word **"Despite"** signals a **contrast**: slow progress ↔ continued optimism → the blank must mean **slow** → **sluggish** ✔
2. Check the others: **painstaking** = very careful/thorough; **rigorous** = strict and thorough; **remarkable** = outstanding.
3. None of these create a contrast with optimism — if progress were remarkable, optimism would be expected, not a surprise.

> 🧠 **MEMORY TRICK — "The blank must fight the second half"**
> Sentences with **despite / although / however / yet / but** require the blank to **oppose** the rest of the sentence:
> - *Despite the **sluggish** progress, they stayed optimistic* ✔
> - *Because of the **remarkable** progress, they stayed optimistic* ✔ (note: "because of", not "despite")
> **Memory line: "Despite = opposite; Because = same."** Check the connector word **before** you look at the options.

## 📌 Section 7 — Verbal Ability Playbook (20 questions in 20 minutes)

| Question type | Time to spend | The one move that wins it |
|---|---|---|
| Reading comprehension | 45 sec | Read the **question first**, then scan for the keyword sentence |
| Main idea | 30 sec | First line = topic, last line = verdict; delete extreme options |
| Direct detail | 30 sec | Match the **exact wording**, not what "sounds sensible" |
| Inference | 45 sec | The answer must be traceable to a line in the passage |
| Para jumble | 40 sec | Chase the **signposts**: first/second, however, therefore, pronouns |
| Odd sentence out | 30 sec | The odd one **changes the subject** |
| Sentence completion | 20 sec | Look for **despite / but / by contrast** → choose the opposite |
| Synonym / antonym | 15 sec | In antonym questions, the **synonyms are the decoys** |
| Idiom | 15 sec | Draw the literal picture |
| One-word substitution | 15 sec | Recall from the memorised list |
| Fill in the blank | 20 sec | Check the **connector word** first |
